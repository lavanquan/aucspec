\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

Batched LLM inference places two service objectives in direct tension:
\emph{system throughput}, the aggregate token rate delivered by the serving
infrastructure (tokens/s), and \emph{user interactivity}, the token rate
experienced by each individual stream (tokens/s/user). Enlarging the
verification batch improves throughput but dilutes per-user rates, while
speculative decoding (SD) accelerates individual streams at the cost of
verification compute that could otherwise serve additional users. In edge
deployments this tension is sharpened by wireless round trips, heterogeneous
draft--target agreement across users, and a hard roofline on the shared
verification server. Existing SD serving systems---in the datacenter and at
the edge---select a \emph{single operating point} on this trade-off, e.g.,
maximum throughput, a fixed latency SLO, or a fairness utility. We instead
treat the entire throughput--interactivity Pareto frontier as the design
object and \emph{maximize its area} (AUC). We formulate AUC maximization as a
bilevel stochastic optimization coupling configuration (draft placement and
sizing) with runtime control (speculation lengths, batching, and wireless
resource allocation), show that the objective admits an operational
robustness interpretation and a tractable dual structure, and lay out the
structural, asymptotic, and online-learning results that the formulation
supports.

\subsection{Network and Speculative Decoding Model}\label{ssec:model}

We consider a set $\cN=\{1,\dots,N\}$ of edge devices, each sustaining a
persistent decode stream, and a shared edge server hosting the target model.
Device $i$ runs a draft model of size $s_i$ selected from a library $\cD$,
with per-token drafting latency $\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}(s_i)$ and memory
footprint $m(s_i)$. Time is slotted for control purposes; stream dynamics
evolve in rounds. In round $k$, stream $i$ drafts
$\gamma_i(k)\in\{0,1,\dots,\gamma_{\max}\}$ tokens, transmits
$\kappa\,\gamma_i(k)$ bits uplink (token identifiers plus quantized draft
probabilities, which suffice for lossless rejection-sampling verification) at
rate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate-wireless}
r_i(t)\;=\;w_i(t)\,\log_2\!\bigl(1+\mathrm{SNR}_i(t)\bigr),
\qquad \textstyle\sum_{i\in\cN} w_i(t)\;\le\; W,
\end{equation}
waits $W_i^{\mathrm{q}}(k)$ in the server queue, is verified within a batch,
and receives the accepted prefix plus one bonus token; $\delta_i$ collects the
fixed per-round latency (downlink, propagation, protocol overhead). The
server verifies a batch $\cB$ carrying
$\Gamma(\cB)=\sum_{i\in\cB}\bigl(\gamma_i+1\bigr)$ tokens in one forward pass.
Table~\ref{tab:notation} summarizes the notation.

We impose the following assumptions before the analysis.

\begin{assumption}[Acceptance process]\label{as:acceptance}
For each stream $i$, token-level acceptance events are i.i.d.\
$\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\alpha_i)$ with $\alpha_i\in[\alpha_{\min},
\alpha_{\max}]\subset(0,1)$, independent across streams; $\alpha_i$ is
\emph{unknown} to the controller. Consequently, a round with speculation
length $\gamma$ delivers $A$ tokens (accepted prefix plus bonus) with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
\E[A]\;=\;\phi(\gamma,\alpha)\;\coloneqq\;\frac{1-\alpha^{\gamma+1}}{1-\alpha},
\end{equation}
and $A-1$ is a geometric random variable truncated at $\gamma$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Roofline verification]\label{as:roofline}
Verifying batch $\cB$ takes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tv}
T^{\mathrm{v}}(\cB)\;=\;\theta_0+\max\Bigl\{\,
\underbrace{\tfrac{b_W+b_{\mathrm{KV}}\sum_{i\in\cB}L_i}{\beta_{\mathrm{mem}}}}_{\text{memory-bound }\theta_m(\cB)},\;
\underbrace{\theta_f\,\Gamma(\cB)}_{\text{compute-bound}}\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
with roofline knee $\Gamma^{\dagger}(\cB)\coloneqq\theta_m(\cB)/\theta_f$ at
which the two regimes cross.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Wireless channels]\label{as:channel}
$\{\mathrm{SNR}_i(t)\}$ are i.i.d.\ across slots (block fading), independent
across streams, and induce bounded rates $0<r_{\min}\le r_i(t)\le r_{\max}$
for any feasible allocation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Closed-loop traffic]\label{as:traffic}
Each stream is persistent and closed-loop: round $k+1$ is issued immediately
upon receipt of round $k$'s response, so per-stream round epochs form renewal
cycles under any stationary policy.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Drafting cost]\label{as:draft}
Drafting $\gamma$ tokens on device $i$ takes $\gamma\,\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}$
seconds and is not overlapped with that stream's verification.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Interactivity, Goodput, and the Achievable Region}\label{ssec:metrics}

Let $\xi\in\Xi$ denote the \emph{configuration}: the draft assignment
$\{s_i\}_{i\in\cN}$, the drafting placement (on-device vs.\ server-side), and
the target replication/parallelism plan. Let $\pi\in\Pi$ denote the
\emph{runtime policy}: the speculation lengths
$\bgamma(t)=(\gamma_1(t),\dots,\gamma_N(t))$, the batch composition
$\cB(t)$, the bandwidth allocation $\bw(t)$, and admission. Denote by
$D_i(t)$ the cumulative tokens delivered to stream $i$ by time $t$; only
accepted and bonus tokens count, so rejected drafts are wasted work that the
control must manage.

\begin{definition}[Interactivity and goodput]\label{def:metrics}
Under a stationary policy $\pi$, by the renewal--reward theorem
(Assumption~\ref{as:traffic}) the \emph{interactivity} of stream $i$ and the
\emph{system goodput} exist almost surely and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi}
x_i(\pi,\xi)\;=\;\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_i(t)}{t}
\;=\;\frac{\phi(\gamma_i,\alpha_i)}
{\E\bigl[\gamma_i\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}+\tfrac{\kappa\gamma_i}{r_i}
+W_i^{\mathrm{q}}+T^{\mathrm{v}}+\delta_i\bigr]},
\qquad
Y(\pi,\xi)\;=\;\sum_{i\in\cN}x_i(\pi,\xi).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Achievable region and frontier]\label{def:frontier}
The achievable region, its frontier, and the max-min interactivity are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:region}
\cC(\xi)\coloneqq\overline{\mathrm{conv}}\,
\bigl\{\bx(\pi,\xi):\pi\in\Pi\ \text{stationary}\bigr\},
\quad
Y^{\star}(x;\xi)\coloneqq\max\Bigl\{\textstyle\sum_{i}x_i:
\bx\in\cC(\xi),\ \min_{i}x_i\ge x\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
for $x\in[0,\bar{x}(\xi)]$ with
$\bar{x}(\xi)\coloneqq\max\{x:\exists\,\bx\in\cC(\xi),\ \min_i x_i\ge x\}$.
Time-sharing between stationary policies attains the convex hull, so every
point of $\cC(\xi)$ is operational.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The AUC Objective and Problem Statement}\label{ssec:problem}

\begin{definition}[Area under the frontier]\label{def:auc}
The AUC of configuration $\xi$ and its normalization are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auc}
\auc(\xi)\;\coloneqq\;\int_{0}^{\bar{x}(\xi)}Y^{\star}(x;\xi)\,dx,
\qquad
\nauc(\xi)\;\coloneqq\;\frac{\auc(\xi)}{\bar{x}(\xi)\,Y^{\star}(0;\xi)}\in(0,1],
\end{equation}
where $Y^{\star}(0;\xi)$ is the unconstrained maximum goodput. $\nauc$ is
unit-free and enables cross-system comparison.
\end{definition}

We propose the following bilevel \emph{frontier-area maximization} problem:
\begin{align}
\textbf{(P)}\qquad
\max_{\xi\in\Xi}\;\; & \auc(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\bar{x}(\xi)}Y^{\star}(x;\xi)\,dx
\label{eq:P}\\
\text{s.t.}\;\;
& m(s_i)\le M_i^{\mathrm{dev}},\quad \forall i\in\cN, \nonumber\\
& b_W \;+\; b_{\mathrm{KV}}\,K^{\max}(\xi)
\;+\;\textstyle\sum_{s\in\text{server drafts}(\xi)}m(s)\;\le\; M^{\mathrm{srv}},
\nonumber
\end{align}
where $K^{\max}(\xi)$ is the KV-cache budget implied by the admissible
concurrency, and the inner value $Y^{\star}(x;\xi)$ is the stochastic network
optimization
\begin{align}
\textbf{(P$_x$)}\qquad
Y^{\star}(x;\xi)=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}\;\;
& \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i\in\cN}\E\bigl[D_i(t)\bigr]
\label{eq:Px}\\
\text{s.t.}\;\;
& \liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{D_i(t)}{t}\;\ge\;x,\quad\forall i\in\cN,
\nonumber\\
& \text{all queues stable};\;\;
\textstyle\sum_i w_i(t)\le W;\;\;
\Gamma(\cB(t))\le\Gamma_{\max},\;\;\forall t. \nonumber
\end{align}

\paragraph{Decision variables and timescales.}
Problem (P) separates naturally into two timescales. The configuration $\xi$
(draft sizing and placement, replication) is set at design time and
\emph{shapes} the frontier; the runtime policy $\pi$ (speculation lengths,
batching, bandwidth, admission) \emph{operates} on it, and the admitted
concurrency is the runtime knob that sweeps the operating point along the
curve. All results below hold for fixed $\xi$; the outer problem is a
combinatorial selection over a profiled or learned surrogate of
$\auc(\cdot)$.

\subsection{AUC Is the Right Objective}\label{ssec:whyauc}

Two results justify the objective in \eqref{eq:P} operationally and
computationally: the first shows that AUC maximization is exactly robust
design against unknown interactivity requirements; the second shows the
frontier---and hence the integral---is computable by a one-dimensional dual
sweep.

\begin{proposition}[Operational meaning of AUC]\label{prop:meaning}
Fix $\xi$ and let $w$ be any probability density on $[0,\bar{x}(\xi)]$. Then
$Y^{\star}(\cdot;\xi)$ is nonincreasing and bounded, hence measurable, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bayes}
\int_{0}^{\bar{x}(\xi)}w(x)\,Y^{\star}(x;\xi)\,dx
\;=\;\E_{X\sim w}\bigl[Y^{\star}(X;\xi)\bigr],
\qquad
\auc(\xi)\;=\;\bar{x}(\xi)\cdot
\E_{X\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,\bar{x}(\xi)]}\bigl[Y^{\star}(X;\xi)\bigr].
\end{equation}
Hence the AUC-optimal configuration is the Bayes-optimal design when the
per-user interactivity requirement is random or unknown with law $w$;
single-point goodput or SLO designs are recovered as the degenerate case
$w=\delta_{x_0}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Monotonicity of $Y^{\star}(\cdot;\xi)$ follows since the feasible set of
\eqref{eq:region} shrinks in $x$; boundedness follows from bounded per-round
rewards and durations (Assumptions~\ref{as:channel}--\ref{as:draft},
$\gamma_i\le\gamma_{\max}$). A bounded monotone function is Borel measurable
and Riemann integrable, and \eqref{eq:bayes} is then the definition of
expectation; the second identity is the uniform special case.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Concavity and dual sweep]\label{prop:dual}
For every $\xi$: (i) $\cC(\xi)$ is convex and compact; (ii)
$Y^{\star}(\cdot;\xi)$ is concave, nonincreasing, and continuous on
$[0,\bar{x}(\xi))$; (iii) for each $x\in[0,\bar{x}(\xi))$ strong duality
holds for \emph{(P$_x$)}, and every frontier point is attained by a
stationary randomized policy solving a \emph{weighted} problem
$\max_{\bx\in\cC(\xi)}\sum_{i}(1+\eta_i)\,x_i$ for some multiplier vector
$\bm{\eta}\in\R_+^N$; (iv) consequently the entire frontier, and the integral
$\auc(\xi)$, are traced by a one-parameter dual path, i.e., a single
max-weight algorithm evaluated along a sweep.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(i) Randomized time-sharing between stationary policies convexifies the rate
set; compactness follows from the bounds in
Proposition~\ref{prop:meaning}'s proof. (ii) For $x=\theta x'+(1-\theta)x''$,
the convex combination of optimizers for $x'$ and $x''$ lies in $\cC(\xi)$
and satisfies the min-rate constraint at $x$, giving
$Y^{\star}(x)\ge\theta Y^{\star}(x')+(1-\theta)Y^{\star}(x'')$; monotonicity
is immediate and concavity implies continuity on the interior. (iii) The
constraint $\min_i x_i\ge x$ is the intersection of $N$ linear constraints;
Slater's condition holds for $x<\bar{x}(\xi)$, so KKT multipliers
$\bm{\eta}(x)$ exist and support the frontier point. (iv) Parametrize by the
support-plane slope; concavity makes the sweep monotone and the integral a
line integral along the dual path.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to GoodSpeed]\label{rem:goodspeed}
The proportional-fair goodput allocation of our prior work
GoodSpeed~\cite{tran2026goodspeed} solves $\max_{\bx\in\cC(\xi)}\sum_i\log
x_i$ and therefore selects a \emph{single} supported point of
$\partial\cC(\xi)$. Problem (P) strictly generalizes it: the log-utility
point is one tangency of the dual sweep in
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}(iv), while (P) optimizes the curve on which that
point lives.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Structural Characterization of the Frontier}\label{ssec:structure}

We first characterize the frontier in the symmetric saturated instance,
which isolates the mechanism generating the Pareto curvature: $\alpha_i=
\alpha$, $\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}=\tau^{\mathrm{d}}$, $r_i=r$, $\delta_i=\delta$,
$b_{\mathrm{KV}}=0$ (so $\theta_m$ is constant), and gated batch service in
which the server synchronously verifies the $B$ admitted streams at a common
speculation length $\gamma$ (so $W_i^{\mathrm{q}}=0$). The per-round cycle
time and the resulting operating point are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Tsym}
T(B,\gamma)=\gamma\Bigl(\tau^{\mathrm{d}}+\tfrac{\kappa}{r}\Bigr)
+\delta+\theta_0+\max\bigl\{\theta_m,\ \theta_f B(\gamma+1)\bigr\},
\qquad
x(B,\gamma)=\frac{\phi(\gamma,\alpha)}{T(B,\gamma)},
\quad
Y=B\,x(B,\gamma).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Two-regime frontier]\label{thm:frontier}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:acceptance}--\ref{as:draft} in the symmetric
saturated instance, with $\Delta T(B,\gamma)\coloneqq
T(B,\gamma+1)-T(B,\gamma)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item $\gamma\mapsto\phi(\gamma,\alpha)$ is strictly increasing and
discretely concave with increments
$\phi(\gamma+1,\alpha)-\phi(\gamma,\alpha)=\alpha^{\gamma+1}$;
\item for each $B$, $\gamma\mapsto x(B,\gamma)$ is unimodal, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gammastar}
\gamma^{\star}(B)\;=\;\max\Bigl\{\gamma\ :\
\alpha^{\gamma+1}\,T(B,\gamma)\;\ge\;\phi(\gamma,\alpha)\,\Delta T(B,\gamma)
\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
i.e., speculate while the relative acceptance gain exceeds the relative
cycle-time cost;
\item $\gamma^{\star}(B)$ is nonincreasing in $B$, and along the frontier
$\gamma^{\star}(x)$ is nondecreasing in $x$: speculation deepens as the
operating point moves toward the interactivity end;
\item \emph{(SD-off threshold)} there exists a finite $B_c$ (equivalently
$x_c>0$) with $\gamma^{\star}(B)=0$ for all $B\ge B_c$: in the
server-bottlenecked compute-bound regime the aggregate goodput equals
$\phi(\gamma,\alpha)\big/\bigl((\gamma+1)\theta_f\bigr)$, and
$\phi(\gamma,\alpha)/(\gamma+1)<1=\phi(0,\alpha)$ for all $\gamma\ge1$ and
$\alpha<1$, so speculation is strictly suboptimal;
\item \emph{(RTT amortization)} $\gamma^{\star}(B)$ is nondecreasing in the
fixed per-round latency $\delta+\theta_0$ and nonincreasing in the per-token
cost $\tau^{\mathrm{d}}+\kappa/r$; high-RTT edge links optimally speculate
deeper at any fixed load.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently $Y^{\star}(\cdot)$ is concave and piecewise smooth with at most
two regimes---memory-bound (high $x$, verification nearly free, speculation
amortizes the wireless round trip) and compute-bound (low $x$, every drafted
token cannibalizes batch capacity)---joined at the roofline knee
$\Gamma^{\dagger}$ of \eqref{eq:Tv}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) is direct from \eqref{eq:phi}. (b): $x(B,\gamma+1)\ge x(B,\gamma)$ iff
$\alpha^{\gamma+1}T(B,\gamma)\ge\phi(\gamma,\alpha)\Delta T(B,\gamma)$; the
left ratio $\alpha^{\gamma+1}/\phi(\gamma,\alpha)$ is strictly decreasing in
$\gamma$ while $\Delta T/T$ is nonincreasing-then-constant, yielding
unimodality and the stopping rule \eqref{eq:gammastar}, a discrete
Dinkelbach condition. (c) and (e) follow from monotone comparative statics
(Topkis) applied to the log-transformed ratio: $B$ and
$\tau^{\mathrm{d}}+\kappa/r$ raise the marginal cost side of
\eqref{eq:gammastar} while $\delta+\theta_0$ raises only the amortizable
fixed cost; composing with the monotone map $x\mapsto B^{\star}(x)$ gives the
frontier statement. (d): in the saturated compute-bound regime the server is
the bottleneck and delivers $\phi(\gamma,\alpha)$ useful tokens per
$(\gamma+1)\theta_f$ seconds of verification; the token-efficiency bound
$\phi(\gamma,\alpha)\le\gamma+1$ with equality only at $\gamma=0$ (for
$\alpha<1$) forces $\gamma^{\star}=0$. Concavity of the frontier is inherited
from Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Edge-specific tension]\label{rem:tension}
Parts (d) and (e) formalize the two opposing forces that make the
\emph{distributed} problem qualitatively different from datacenter SD:
wireless round-trip amortization pushes $\gamma$ up while verification
contention pushes it down, so the optimal policy is spatially heterogeneous
even for identical acceptance rates, and the sign of the net effect flips
across the frontier.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Fluid limit and finite-$N$ gap]\label{thm:fluid}
Consider the sequence of systems indexed by $N$ with server token rate
scaled as $c_N=cN$ and i.i.d.\ stream parameters. Then the empirical measure
of stream states converges, uniformly on compacts and in probability, to the
unique fixed point of a fluid model; $Y_N^{\star}(x)/N\to y^{\star}(x)$ for a
closed-form limiting frontier $y^{\star}$; and
$\sup_{x}\bigl|Y_N^{\star}(x)/N-y^{\star}(x)\bigr|=O(1/\sqrt{N})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof strategy]
The closed system of $N$ circulating rounds with think times (drafting plus
uplink) and a roofline batch server is a density-dependent Markov chain;
Kurtz's theorem and exchangeability (propagation of chaos) give the fluid
limit, and a functional CLT gives the $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ rate. This extends the
fluid sample-path analysis of GoodSpeed~\cite{tran2026goodspeed} from a
single log-utility operating point to the parametric family
$\{(\mathrm{P}_x)\}_{x\in[0,\bar{x}]}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Online Distributed Control with Censored Learning}\label{ssec:control}

We now solve (P$_x$) online, without knowledge of $\{\alpha_i\}$, by a
drift-plus-penalty controller whose per-slot subproblems decouple across
devices, server, and radio. Let $\Delta_{\mathrm{s}}$ be the slot length and
$a_i(t)$ the tokens delivered to stream $i$ in slot $t$. Define the
interactivity, server-compute, and device-time virtual queues
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vq}
Z_i(t{+}1)=\bigl[Z_i(t)+x\Delta_{\mathrm{s}}-a_i(t)\bigr]^{+},\quad
Q(t{+}1)=\bigl[Q(t)+\theta_f\Gamma(\cB(t))-\Delta_{\mathrm{s}}\bigr]^{+},\quad
Q_i(t{+}1)=\Bigl[Q_i(t)+\gamma_i(t)\bigl(\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}
+\tfrac{\kappa}{r_i(t)}\bigr)-\Delta_{\mathrm{s}}\Bigr]^{+},
\end{equation}
and the prices $\lambda(t)\coloneqq Q(t)$ and $\mu_i(t)\coloneqq Q_i(t)$.
Minimizing the one-slot drift-plus-penalty bound decouples into three
subproblems.

\paragraph{(i) Device-side speculation index.}
The marginal benefit of the $(\gamma{+}1)$-th draft token is
$(V+Z_i(t))\,\alpha_i^{\gamma+1}$ expected deliverable tokens, while its
marginal cost is $\lambda(t)\theta_f+\mu_i(t)\bigl(\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}+
\kappa/r_i(t)\bigr)$; optimal stopping yields the closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:index}
\gamma_i(t)\;=\;\Bigl[\,\Bigl\lceil
\tfrac{\ln c_i(t)}{\ln\hat{\alpha}_i(t)}\Bigr\rceil-1\Bigr]_{0}^{\gamma_{\max}},
\qquad
c_i(t)\;=\;\frac{\lambda(t)\,\theta_f
+\mu_i(t)\bigl(\tau_i^{\mathrm{d}}+\kappa/r_i(t)\bigr)}{V+Z_i(t)},
\end{equation}
where $[\,\cdot\,]_{0}^{\gamma_{\max}}$ clamps to $\{0,\dots,\gamma_{\max}\}$
and $\hat{\alpha}_i(t)$ is the current (optimistic) acceptance estimate.
The server broadcasts the single scalar $\lambda(t)$---a posted
\emph{verification-token price}---and devices respond selfishly, so the
architecture is one-scalar-feedback network utility maximization.

\paragraph{(ii) Server-side batching.}
Batch selection is the token-budget knapsack
$\max_{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\omega_i(t)$ s.t.\
$\Gamma(\mathcal{S})\le\Gamma_{\mathrm{bud}}(t)$ with weights
$\omega_i(t)=(V+Z_i(t))\,\phi(\gamma_i,\hat{\alpha}_i)$ over pending
verifications; the greedy rule ranked by $\omega_i/(\gamma_i+1)$ is optimal
for the LP relaxation and within one job of integer-optimal, with
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{bud}}(t)$ set to the knee $\Gamma^{\dagger}$ (memory-bound
preserving) or driven by $Q(t)$.

\paragraph{(iii) Radio allocation.}
Given per-stream time prices, minimizing
$\sum_i\mu_i(t)\kappa\gamma_i(t)/\bigl(w_i g_i(t)\bigr)$ with
$g_i(t)=\log_2(1+\mathrm{SNR}_i(t))$ subject to $\sum_i w_i\le W$ admits the
square-root waterfilling solution
$w_i^{\star}(t)=W\sqrt{\mu_i\kappa\gamma_i/g_i}\big/
\sum_{j}\sqrt{\mu_j\kappa\gamma_j/g_j}$ by Cauchy--Schwarz.

\begin{theorem}[Controller optimality]\label{thm:dpp}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:acceptance}--\ref{as:draft} with known
$\{\alpha_i\}$, for any $V>0$ the controller
\eqref{eq:vq}--\eqref{eq:index} achieves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dppbound}
\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\sum_{\tau\le t}\E\Bigl[\sum_i a_i(\tau)\Bigr]
\;\ge\;Y^{\star}(x;\xi)-\frac{C}{V},
\qquad
\E\bigl[Z_i(t)+Q(t)+Q_i(t)\bigr]=O(V)\ \ \forall i,
\end{equation}
so every stream's time-average interactivity satisfies its constraint within
$O(V/t)$, for a constant $C$ determined by the bounded second moments of
per-slot rewards and durations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Quadratic Lyapunov function on $(\mathbf{Z},Q,\mathbf{Q})$; the per-slot
minimizer of the drift-plus-penalty bound is exactly
(i)--(iii); boundedness of $\gamma_{\max}$, $r_{\max}$, and \eqref{eq:Tv}
gives the constant $C$; the $[O(1/V),O(V)]$ trade-off follows from the
standard telescoping argument, and Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}(iii)
guarantees a stationary randomized policy achieving $Y^{\star}(x;\xi)$ to
compare against.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Censored learning of acceptance rates]\label{thm:learning}
Let $\{\alpha_i\}$ be unknown in $[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]$. Each round
with $\gamma_i\ge1$ reveals a truncated-geometric observation: the accepted
prefix contributes Bernoulli successes and, unless all $\gamma_i$ drafts are
accepted (right-censoring), one failure. Running \eqref{eq:index} with
upper-confidence estimates $\hat{\alpha}_i^{\mathrm{ucb}}(t)$ built from
Bernstein confidence intervals on these samples yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret}
\mathrm{Reg}(T)\;\coloneqq\;T\,Y^{\star}(x;\xi)
-\sum_{t\le T}\E\Bigl[\sum_i a_i(t)\Bigr]
\;=\;\widetilde{O}\bigl(\sqrt{NT}\bigr)+O\bigl(T/V\bigr),
\end{equation}
while preserving the $O(V)$ backlog guarantees of Theorem~\ref{thm:dpp}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each informative round contributes at least one Bernoulli sample, so
$|\hat{\alpha}_i-\alpha_i|=\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{n_i})$ by Bernstein; the
index \eqref{eq:index} and $\phi(\cdot,\alpha)$ are Lipschitz in $\alpha$ on
the compact $[\alpha_{\min},\alpha_{\max}]$; optimism bounds per-round regret
by the confidence width, and summing $\sqrt{1/n_i}$ over streams and rounds
gives $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{NT})$; the $O(T/V)$ term is the controller gap of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dpp}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Self-exploring constraint pressure]\label{rem:explore}
Rounds with $\gamma_i=0$ are uninformative, an earning-while-learning
obstruction absent from unconstrained bandit formulations. The controller
resolves it endogenously: whenever stream $i$'s interactivity constraint
binds, $Z_i(t)$ grows, $c_i(t)\downarrow0$ in \eqref{eq:index}, and
$\gamma_i(t)\ge1$ is forced---constraint pressure guarantees exploration, and
explicit forced exploration is needed only for streams with slack
constraints.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Extensions}\label{ssec:extensions}

\begin{remark}[Multi-server routing via posted prices]
With $M$ verification servers each posting a price $\lambda_m(t)$, devices
jointly select $(m,\gamma)$ by the natural extension of \eqref{eq:index}. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}(i) the aggregate problem remains convex, so the
posted-price equilibrium coincides with the dual optimum and routing incurs
no efficiency loss; the transient is a congestion-game dynamic amenable to
standard analysis.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Hierarchical draft cascades]
A device tiny-draft $\to$ mid-tier verify-and-redraft $\to$ target cascade is
a tandem of speculative batch-service queues; characterizing the composed
frontier is open and maps directly onto a Pi~5 $\to$ Orin $\to$ DGX-class
testbed hierarchy.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Long-context regime]
With $b_{\mathrm{KV}}>0$ in \eqref{eq:Tv}, the memory floor grows with
$\sum_{i\in\cB}L_i$ and the knee $\Gamma^{\dagger}$ shifts right, so
speculation remains optimal at larger batch sizes for long contexts;
Assumption~\ref{as:roofline} captures this without modification.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Position within prior art]
Datacenter systems select speculation lengths to maximize a goodput or SLO
objective at the \emph{current} operating
point~\cite{liu2024smartspec,li2025adaserve,adaspec2025,banditspec2025}, and
edge systems architect distributed drafting and verification around
throughput or SLO
targets~\cite{sled2025,dsd2025,dssd2025,wisp2026,configspec2026}. In the
language of Proposition~\ref{prop:meaning}, all are the degenerate case
$w=\delta_{x_0}$ of \eqref{eq:bayes}; none characterizes or optimizes the
frontier, couples wireless resource allocation with speculation control, or
handles censored acceptance learning with guarantees.
\end{remark}
